The Concept

In 1967, John Lennon took the words and characters from a Victorian circus poster and turned them into Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite! on The Beatles’ album Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band. Pablo Fanque’s Circus Royal takes that creative idea further: an entire concept album inspired by Pablo Fanque’s posters.

What if the poster had been the beginning of a whole album?

One playbill opened the door. Others revealed a larger world of ringmasters, acrobats, clowns, horses and extraordinary attractions. Their names and extravagant promises became starting points for new songs, imagining the people, dreams and struggles behind the spectacle.

Presented in three acts and eighteen original songs, the album follows an evening at Circus Royal—from Pablo’s welcome to the performers’ final bow. Victorian characters meet the adventurous spirit of 1967, with music that finds its own voice.

This is an expansion of Lennon’s poster-inspired approach into a standalone album of original music, storytelling and visual art. Step inside and discover how much life can unfold from the words on a circus poster.

The inspiration belongs to 1967, the characters to the 1800s, and the music to the present.
